# Design

## Context

- `GridLayout` (`alttab-native.ahk`) wraps tiles into centred rows and shrinks the thumbnails to `TILE_MIN` when the rows don't fit. Beyond that it shows `grid.vis` whole rows from `grid.top`. `TileY`, `Shown`, `Reveal` and `PaneWheel` all work in whole rows.
- `ThumbsShow` unregisters every DWM thumbnail on each scroll and registers the shown tiles' again. DWM draws thumbnails over our surface, so nothing we paint can clip or fade them; only their properties can (`rcDestination`, `rcSource`, `fVisible`, opacity).
- `Render` redraws the whole surface on each change (about 1.6 ms for 20 tiles). The picture's slide already animates on a 15 ms timer (`PeekTick`, ease-out cubic).
- Mouse handlers only note events and timers act on them, because posted messages run even inside a Critical hotkey. The pane is never the foreground window, and Windows lets only the foreground window capture the mouse, so a drag gets no `WM_MOUSEMOVE` once the pointer leaves the pane.
- The picture rises with the window count until `MANY_FROM + 4` windows (`PeekShare`; `MANY_FROM` is a user setting).

## Decisions

- **One scroller for both layouts: a pixel offset.** `grid.top` and `grid.vis` give way to an offset into the content and a goal offset. A tile's screen y is its content y minus the offset. The 1-8 layout keeps its wrapping and shrinking; on the rare screen where it still overflows, it uses the same scroller. Rejected: animating between whole rows, which can't follow a drag.
- **Grid tiles.** From `GRID_FROM := 9` windows, tiles go in `GRID_COLS := 3` columns, with the last row left-aligned so the columns stay put. A tile is as tall as an unshrunk tile today (header plus the `TILE_H` slot). Its slot has the work area's shape, clamped to `ASPECT_MIN`..`ASPECT_MAX`, so a maximized window fills its tile and others are letterboxed. The slot narrows only when 3 don't fit in `PANE_MAX_W`. `grid.rows` keeps its form, so `Nearest` and `StepRow` already give Up/Down by column. Rejected: tiles shaped like the widest window, since one wide window would widen them all.
- **Rows sized for the picture's highest peek.** The grid's row count comes from the room under the art at `PEEK_MAX`, not at the current count, so the pane keeps its height while the picture rises. That art is the tallest of the pictures that can show from `GRID_FROM` windows (moods `MoodOf(GRID_FROM)` .. 3), so a custom `MANY_FROM` inside the grid range can't change the height. The pane is the full rows plus the top and bottom padding, and it goes under that same art, so it doesn't move as the picture rises or after a close.
- **Rows are cut at the pane's edge, not at the padding.** Clip just inside the drawn edge so its lip stays on top. Resting positions keep the selected row inside the padding, so the padding shows part of the neighbouring rows. That is the overflow hint, and it stays after the bar fades. Rejected: cutting at the padding's inner line, which makes rows vanish mid-glass and leaves a hard cut floating inside the pane after a drag.
- **Thumbnails: registered on first sight, cropped each frame, hidden instead of released.** For a partly visible tile, `rcDestination` is its fitted rect cut to the viewport, and `rcSource` is cut by the same fractions. Always set `DWM_TNP_RECTSOURCE`, also for minimized windows (use the queried source size). Compute in floating point and round once. A tile that leaves the view gets `fVisible = FALSE`. Thumbnails are unregistered only when the pane closes or lays out again after a close. Rejected: registering every tile at open, which puts up to 50 calls into DWM on the Alt+Tab path (budget ≤ 30 ms); and today's re-registering on each scroll, which means churn on every frame and a blank frame.
- **Animation: one frame timer toward a goal that can change.** `SCROLL_MS := 120`, with the picture's `ANIM_TICK` and ease-out cubic. A new goal (another Tab, another notch) restarts the ease from the current offset, and notches add up on the goal. Each frame does the offset, hover, thumbnails, `Render` and `Present` back to back. The first frame runs at once, as in `PeekTo`. On opening, the grid starts at the offset that shows the selection, without animation (Shift+Tab opens at the bottom).
- **Scroll bar, drawn in our surface.** Its width and opacity ease on the frame timer, and "near" means the mouse is in the right padding. It shows on opening and on each scroll, and a one-shot timer about 1 s later starts a short fade. Its colour goes in both palettes (`bar`: the text colour at partial alpha, as WinUI's). No arrow buttons or track clicks, since neither was asked for. A drag starts with a press on the thumb. The frame timer then polls the cursor and the physical button with `GetAsyncKeyState`, minding swapped buttons, until release.
- **Mouse in content coordinates.** Each point is noted with the offset at that moment (content y = client y + offset), so `OnLayout` only compares layouts and `HitTest` works in content coordinates. A click during a scroll then hits what was under the pointer at press and at release, and does nothing if they differ. Hover is recomputed each frame from the last mouse position.
- **A press keeps the glass live.** On the first real press, Windows draws the never-activated pane as inactive and its acrylic turns flat grey (found on today's code; the WM_NCACTIVATE(TRUE) of a re-layout has hidden it so far, since clicks switch or close). A drag starts with such a press, so while open the pane keeps its active look through a press.
- **A close keeps the offset,** clamped to the new end, and scrolls only if the selection is out of view. Thumbnails start afresh after that new layout, as today.
- **Closed means idle.** `PaneFree` stops the scroll, fade and drag timers and resets their state.
- **Testability.** `grid` exposes the offset and the goal, so the test can check where a scroll is heading without waiting for frames. `GridLayout` takes an optional DPI scale (default: the screen's), so the test can check 1080p at 150 % on the 100 % dev screen.

## Risks / Trade-offs

- [Thumbnail updates and our pixels go through separate calls and can land one DWM frame apart, so headers and thumbnails may shear mid-scroll.] → Update both back to back in each frame and keep frames short. A 120 ms scroll is about 8 frames; judge it by eye on the dev machine.
- [AutoHotkey timers tick about every 15.6 ms.] → The picture's slide uses the same rate and looks smooth. Raising the timer resolution is out of scope.
- [A cut row has a hard edge, less soft than the roadmap's fade.] → The fade moved to Velvet (item 3), at the author's choice.
- [The simulated 150 % layout takes header heights from the 100 % font, a few px off.] → The fit check is the same arithmetic at any scale, so the test still catches a pane that doesn't fit under the picture.
